# Postack Underflow — an open experiment in collective memory

**Working concept · 28 October 2026 · not a product pitch**

> **How does knowledge get found, connected, challenged, and remembered?**

## Origin and argument
The trigger is [Syntax #1050](https://syntax.fm/show/1050/everyone-is-hacking-their-tv/transcript): the contrast between a few search words finding a public Stack Overflow answer and agents potentially reconstructing solutions in isolated sessions. This is a provocation, not proof of general decline.

Two coupled but distinct hypotheses:
- **Discovery:** previous descriptions and classification work determine whether an existing solution is findable and applicable.
- **Community:** shared knowledge needs correction, disagreement, trust, continuity and stewardship—not merely portable storage.

Their possible feedback loop is **publish → discover → reuse → challenge → correct → maintain → rediscover**. It remains a hypothesis.

## Platform as an experimental medium
Building a “platform” here means tinkering with working software to think: testing index structures, ATProto records, contextual interfaces, and surprising failures. It does not imply a startup, business model, or new social-network destination.

Three valid outcomes:
1. **Research success:** we learn something, including from negative results.
2. **Creative success:** an interaction reveals a relationship previously invisible.
3. **Practical success:** a measured improvement in retrieval, interpretation, or reuse.

Only the third supports performance claims. Keep **imagined / implemented / demonstrated** visibly distinct.

## What the critical research changed
Prior art such as doc2query, BM25F, code search, bug localization and existing publication protocols should become baselines and inspiration, not reasons to stop exploring. The critique correctly warns that public Q&A displacement does not prove net knowledge loss; added vocabulary may explain Four Words gains; portability does not manufacture community; and curation has costs.

**Four Words V1 remains frozen.** Its post-hoc audit found vocabulary and relevance-label weaknesses. No retrospective rewriting of its metrics or corpus.

## Physical-object research as method
The independent [Lumpesammlig](https://github.com/blackmath88/lumpesammlig) studies are encounters, not Postack components. The card catalogue reveals that classification is prior labour, governed interpretation, and imperfect access. The switchboard reveals accountable routing, mediation, temporary commitment and release. Their possible convergence is **attributable claims over independently inspectable sources**: classification claims and relationship claims can be questioned without rewriting source material.

## Storytelling position
Start with a *concrete public hack or shared finding*, not a product feature. A candidate is a digital-picture-frame hacking repository reportedly mentioned in Syntax #1050. Attribution and repo are **not verified**; see [case-study lead](research/CASE-STUDY-LEADS.md). Show how a curious hack becomes discoverable knowledge—or fails to.

The journey: **encounter → lost shortcut → indexing rabbit hole → community rabbit hole → possible Problem Desk convergence → the article indexes its own research history**.

The Problem Desk is a local experimental interface in which a contribution proposes an index correction; retrieval changes in preview, but the original source remains intact. It is not proof of federation or a new commons.

## Working north star
**We already solved this. But can we find it, understand it, challenge it, and leave it better for the next person?**

Build strange things. Let them challenge the theory. Document what we discover.

No global crawler, central reputation score, automatic consensus, premature monetization, or Weavr mission scope expansion is implied.
