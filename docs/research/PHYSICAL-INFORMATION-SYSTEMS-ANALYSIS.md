# Analysis — Physical information systems research

**Status:** internal interpretation of user-supplied external research; not an independently verified historical literature review.  
**Structured source:** [PHYSICAL-INFORMATION-SYSTEMS.json](PHYSICAL-INFORMATION-SYSTEMS.json)

## Executive assessment

The report materially improves our conception of both artifacts. Its strongest contribution is not a new visual reference but a new **unit of accountability**: *classification claims* for the Index and *relationship claims* for the Switchboard. These are independently attributable, revisable layers over source records. That distinction connects the two rabbit holes without conflating them.

The strongest editorial proposition is **“The interface is allowed to change its reading, not somebody else's source.”** It can become a testable action, not just a slogan.

## Five consequential findings

1. **Indexing is governance, not just precomputation.** The NLM retaining-rod example introduces a crucial difference between provisional and accepted placement. This maps directly to Postack's Four Words V1 weakness: aliases and classifications introduced by the record writer can improve matching while being unsupported. The answer is not more decorative cards; it is to expose source-groundedness, who proposed a heading, and what accepting it would change.
2. **Stable neighbourhoods versus ranked lists.** Query ranking can hide the collection's existing structure. An index needs browseable scope, cross-references, and preserved unmatched records. The research corrects an overemphasis on accession order: that is not the same as the historical public filing system.
3. **The Switchboard is an operational lifecycle.** The current “unplug all” interaction is useful, but incomplete. A connection should be inspectable as proposed → established → supervised/contested → withdrawn. Not all relationships should require the same lifecycle; an author-declared reply is different from a viewer's speculative link.
4. **The historical analogy has a boundary.** A switchboard routes live speech; it does not preserve posts. Persistent source records are our *digital transgression*. We should state this explicitly, or the metaphor becomes historically misleading.
5. **The convergence is maintenance, not a master graph.** The proposed Problem Desk is valuable only if a community contribution can produce a **previewable index correction** without changing the original contribution. This is the strongest place where the two problems genuinely interact.

## Relationship to existing Postack experiments

### Four Words V1
The frozen V1 evaluation must stay frozen. The external report does not independently validate its results. It does suggest a valuable **separate V2**: compare baseline retrieval with an index that exposes claim-level evidence and allows proposed corrections. Avoid retroactively editing V1's labels, aliases, or rankings. Test unsupported alias rates and whether users can distinguish source text from writer-supplied vocabulary.

### Community fixture
Preserve the existing eight source contributions and the off-by-default model-suggested connections. Add provenance and relationship-class distinction *in a new experiment*, not by silently redefining existing fixture evidence. “Unplug all” must not imply deleting authors' records or guaranteed persistence on independent hosts.

### Editorial narrative
Use the material encounter as an **explanatory lens**, not the product architecture. The visitor should first encounter a real, documented discovery failure, then see the classification or routing mechanism. The essay can end with one Problem Desk interaction, but no third machine is necessary.

## Recommended sequence — deliberately narrow

| Priority | Experiment | Why now | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disputed Neighbour** | Makes the Four Words unsupported-alias finding understandable through one concrete false equivalence | No change to frozen V1 |
| 2 | **Connection Contract + Operator Leaves** | Distinguishes author-declared relations from editorial/AI proposals and makes local view authority legible | No inferred author endorsement |
| 3 | **Problem Desk** | Demonstrates a contribution proposing a classification change and previewing retrieval effects | Fixture-only, proposal never auto-accepted |
| Later | **Above the Rod** | Deepens governance and staged acceptance | Avoid bureaucratic theatre |
| Independent study | **Held Place / Limited Desk** | Rich embodied/haptic hypotheses with uncertain product value | Compare against simpler interfaces |

The first two can be developed separately and measured against a boring baseline. The third is justified only after they work independently.

## Design implications

The strongest material translation is not wooden textures or dangling cables. It is **interaction friction in the right place**: no friction for reading or undoing, deliberate confirmation for publishing a relationship claim or accepting an index change. Motion should reveal reclassification or connection changes, never become the argument.

The report proposes serif editorial reading text, while our current direction favors sans. This is an **open visual experiment**, not a reason to override the approved art direction. Test a small specimen with typography serving source/claim distinctions before changing the site globally.

The proposed blue/violet/rose state semantics are plausible but need contrast tests and redundant non-color indicators. A rose line should never be the only way a reader recognizes disagreement.

## Research cautions

- The supplied report contains many citations and historical claims; this ingestion records them as **reported**, not independently source-verified. Verify NLM retaining-rod details, switchboard sequence/capacity, and institutional catalogue filing before presenting them as historical facts on the public site.
- The report's Syntax transcript caveat is in tension with earlier conversation claims that the relevant discussion was found. Verify the precise passage before quoting or revising attribution.
- The report mentions source SHA prefixes from its own inspection. They are not treated as verified current repository revisions here.
- Avoid treating an editorially attractive mechanism as proof of retrieval effectiveness or federation.
- The proposed Problem Desk risks becoming a complex administrative interface. A single 60–90 second worked example should be the first test.
- The source report's 90-item reference list contains duplicates and variable-quality sources; the JSON intentionally preserves a **selected primary/relevant reference set**, not a claim that all 90 were independently audited.

## Falsifiable prototype acceptance

A reader should be able to answer, without reading documentation:

1. What did the original source actually say?
2. Who assigned this problem description or relationship?
3. Why was this connection made, and is it author-declared or viewer-proposed?
4. What changes when I remove the current reading?
5. What would change if a proposed classification were accepted?

If a plain textual comparison answers these better than the experimental UI, prefer the plain comparison.

## Decision

**Adopt as research input, not as an approved implementation specification.** Keep the two independent Lumpesammlig encounters independent. Use Postack to test *claim accountability* as the bridge between indexing and community. Any new UI implementation, protocol write, publication or benchmark run needs its own scoped decision and evidence.
